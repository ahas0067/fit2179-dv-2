Australian Basketball Participation

How strong is Basketball Participation across Australia?

Basketball is one of Australia's major participation sports. This visualisation explores where the game is strongest, who plays it, and how organised participation and facilities shape access to the sport.

Where Basketball is Strongest

Basketball participation is not distributed evenly across Australia. This section compares where basketball has the strongest participation rate, then checks whether high-rate states also have the largest player bases.

Victoria leads by participation rate

The choropleth map compares derived total basketball participation rates across states and territories. Darker states represent a higher share of the population participating in basketball. This gives a geographic view of where basketball participation is strongest relative to population size.

High participation does not always mean the biggest player base

The bubble scatter plot compares each state's estimated number of basketball participants with its derived participation rate. This separates two different ideas: how popular basketball is relative to population, and how many total players each state contributes.

Who Plays Basketball?

Basketball participation also differs across population groups. Looking at adults and children separately shows whether the sport is driven more by broad adult participation or by younger players entering the game.

Children participate at a much higher rate than adults

The rate comparison shows that basketball is especially strong among children. Adults still make up a large number of total participants, but children participate at a much higher rate relative to their population size.

Gender participation shows another layer of difference

The diverging bar chart compares male and female basketball participation rates. Instead of only showing total participant counts, this view focuses on participation rate, making the comparison fairer across different population sizes.

Teenage groups dominate basketball participation

The lollipop chart compares basketball participation rates across age groups. The dot marks the participation rate for each age group, while the line shows how far that group extends from zero. This makes the teenage peak easier to spot without treating age groups as a continuous time trend.

How Basketball is Played

Participation is not only about how many people play, but also about how they play. This section looks at whether basketball participation is structured through organised settings or played more casually.

Adult basketball is almost evenly split between organised and non-organised play

The donut chart compares organised and non-organised adult basketball participation. The split is close to even, showing that adult basketball is not only club-based or competition-based, but also includes a large casual participation base.

Children rely more heavily on clubs and schools

The connected dot plot compares organiser types for adult and child basketball participation. The distance between the dots shows where children and adults rely on different participation settings, such as clubs, schools, recreation centres, coaches and private providers.

Where Can Basketball Be Played?

Participation also depends on access to places to play. This section looks at recorded basketball facilities in Victoria to see where facility availability is strongest.

Some Victorian LGAs record far more basketball facilities than others

The ranked bar chart compares the Victorian Local Government Areas (LGAs) with the highest number of recorded basketball facilities. This helps show where basketball infrastructure is most concentrated, rather than only focusing on participation rates.

Facilities cluster heavily around Melbourne and regional centres

The dot map shows the location of recorded Victorian basketball facilities. Each dot represents a facility, with larger dots showing venues with more estimated courts. This adds a spatial view of access, showing where facilities are physically concentrated.
